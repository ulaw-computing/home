Why Fonts Matter

By Priya Shah, design editor

Most people never notice the fonts they read every day. That is exactly what a good font is designed to do.

Invisible by Design

A well-chosen font does not draw attention to itself. Instead, it lets the reader focus on the words. When a font is badly chosen, readers slow down, lose their place and, on the web, often give up and leave the page altogether.

Designers judge body text fonts by how comfortable they are to read for long periods, not by how striking they look in a single word. The TypeMaster Pro software that many designers once relied on has long been replaced by free web fonts.

Good typography is invisible. Bad typography is everywhere.

Choosing Fonts for the Web

On screen, fonts with simple shapes, open letters and generous spacing tend to be easiest to read. Many sites use one font for headings, chosen for personality, and a second, plainer font for body text, chosen for comfort.

Size and spacing matter just as much as the font itself. Text that is too small, or lines that are packed too tightly, can make even the best font hard work to read.
